3D Reconstruction of Developing Auditory Neural Circuits

Hearing depends on precise connections between neurons, and those connections take shape as the brain develops. I turned stacks of confocal microscope images into 3D models of neurons and their synapses, so the lab could study how the auditory pathway wires itself.

Role
Undergraduate research assistant
Lab
Auditory Development & Connectomics Laboratory
When
2023
Tools
Confocal microscopy data, Seg3D, syGlass

01Overview

To hear, neurons along the auditory pathway have to connect to the right partners, and those connections are built and refined during development. Understanding how that happens means looking at the circuits themselves: the shape of each neuron and where its synapses are.

A confocal microscope images tissue one thin optical slice at a time. Each slice shows only a cross-section of every cell, so a neuron's real shape only appears once the slices are put back together and the cell is traced in 3D.

02Objective

Reconstruct neurons and their synaptic structures from high-resolution confocal datasets, accurately and the same way every time, so they could be measured and compared across development.

03My contribution

  • Analyzed high-resolution confocal microscopy datasets to reconstruct more than 600 neurons and synaptic structures in the developing auditory pathway.
  • Built 3D visualizations of neuron shape in Seg3D and syGlass, which made axon and dendrite tracing more accurate.
  • Helped with image preprocessing, quality control and data annotation, keeping the pipeline consistent across large microscopy datasets.
  • Contributed to quantitative analyses of synapse density, spatial organization and structural variability.

04Design process

Every dataset went through the same pipeline, from a raw stack of images to a 3D model that could be measured. Scroll through it below.

One slice

A cross-section of each cell. Branches leave the slice and turn up again in others, so it is easy to lose track of which piece belongs to which neuron.

The 3D model

The whole neuron, with its branches joined up and its synapses in place, ready to be measured.

05Testing & validation

A reconstruction is only useful if it is accurate and done the same way every time, because small tracing errors add up across hundreds of cells. Seeing the tracings in 3D made mistakes easier to spot than they are slice by slice, such as a branch that wanders onto a neighboring cell.

  • Preprocessing cleaned up and aligned the images before any tracing started.
  • Annotation followed the same rules for every neuron and synapse, so the results could be compared.
  • Quality control checked the tracings before they went into the analysis.

06Results

600+neurons and synaptic structures reconstructed
3Dmodels of neuron shape, built in Seg3D and syGlass

The reconstructions fed the lab's quantitative analyses of synapse density, spatial organization and structural variability, which support its hypotheses on how auditory pathways mature.

07Key takeaways

  • Consistency is what scales. Across hundreds of neurons, doing every step the same way matters as much as any single tracing.
  • 3D shows what slices hide. A neuron that looks like scattered pieces in one slice makes sense once it is rebuilt in three dimensions.
  • A foundation for later research. Careful image analysis and a consistent pipeline are habits I carried into R&D at Axogen.
