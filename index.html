This project focused on developing a non-invasive oral interface for anesthesia-free infant flexible laryngoscopy. The goal was to improve scope guidance, oral cavity fit, and safety during pediatric airway evaluation.

My role included CAD design, prototype refinement, benchtop testing, and design iteration based on clinician feedback. I evaluated scope insertion pathways, structural stability, manufacturability, material behavior, and infant oral cavity fit to support future clinical translation.

I also developed manufacturing documentation and benchtop test protocols aligned with ISO 13485 and ISO 14971 principles, maintaining traceability across six prototype iterations to support manufacturability and future LSR molding scale-up.

BME Laboratory · USF · Spring 2025

Wearable that detects and corrects unsafe lifting posture

Sensors
EMG · IMU · flex
Lifting trials
45+
Detection accuracy
> 90%
Feedback

Lifting injuries are a leading cause of back pain, and people rarely get feedback on their form in the moment. We built a vest that reads erector spinae muscle activity and back angle, then buzzes when posture turns unsafe.

  • Integrated EMG electrodes, an IMU and a flex sensor on an Arduino, driving a vibration motor through a transistor stage.
  • Built a MATLAB pipeline to filter EMG and classify safe versus unsafe lifts across heights and postures.
  • Wrote the test protocols and used the trial data to guide each prototype iteration. Presented as a poster at the USF BME Laboratory I Showcase.

Embedded Systems · USF

Merry Migraine Minder: a blue-light exposure sensor

Sensor
AS7341, 8-channel spectral
Band of interest
400–500 nm
White phone screen, normal mode
up to ~40% blue
Warm indoor lighting

Short-wavelength light is a known migraine trigger, but there's no simple way to measure everyday "blue-light load." This team project turned a spectral sensor into a live migraine-risk monitor.

  • Read the AS7341 datasheet and wrote Arduino I²C code to compute a blue ratio relative to total visible light.
  • Built a Python Tkinter GUI that reads the serial stream and flags readings against migraine-risk thresholds.
  • Designed a power-regulated PCB in KiCad and tested phone screens across brightness levels and night mode, plus five floors of the USF library.
